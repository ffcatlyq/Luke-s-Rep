Video content focuses on the emotion as well as the subject and looks and feels like the updated [photographic style](https://branding.roche.com/document/26#/photography-and-video/photography-guidelines) we have created. The footage encompasses all attributes and brings human and empathetic feelings to the fore.

The storyline is determined by your briefing, whereas the execution depends very much on the processes of storyboarding, to bring the narrative closer to the audience.

Through moving imagery, we can capture the connection to our audiences that photography simply cannot achieve. Our video material should be filmed with the simplest of structures. We aim to communicate in an elegant, authentic and simple fashion without the need for special effects.

As capturing moments may require multiple takes, it is recommended to work in a slow and meaningful manner to capture the best footage, less is more. Repetition of scenes or processes may not be possible, so planning is vital.

Slow-motion brings calmness to a scene, so capture scenes at a higher frame rate when slow-motion is deemed appropriate.

B-Roll is a key part of any production. Make sure to take enough B-Roll material to fill in the gaps, add tension or more detailed moments to be added in the video editing process. This will ultimately help to engage the viewer for longer.
Roche moving imagery is captured using the latest equipment to ensure the best possible level of quality. Filming in 4K is preferred, as it gives the flexibility to crop in without issues. In addition, ungraded footage is required to ensure the material can be adjusted and compensated.

If the content you are working on is for a digital platform, you can consider using 1080p HD if losing the ability to crop/zoom afterwards is not an issue. Mobile phone footage is acceptable if it has been captured with up-to-date equipment and is deemed the appropriate method.

Your footage should be stabilised either by using a camera with in-body 5-axis stabilisation or with equipment like a slider or gimbal. Post-processing can achieve smooth footage, but use this only as a backup.

Lighting is to be kept as natural as possible. The use of modern lighting gear is permitted, though some locations might not allow it or have enough available space. Scouting before your production is critical.

Where possible, audio should be captured using a microphone boom. Wireless lavaliere microphones can also be used, whilst paying close attention to clothing and their positioning. Check your location for ambient noise levels in advance. Some locations have noisy ventilation, equipment that cannot be turned off and external noises such as building works, which cannot be controlled and can jeopardise the audio quality. Again, scouting before your production is critical.

Please keep a copy of all your data as an archival backup (made available upon request) for as long as your contract with Roche is valid.
