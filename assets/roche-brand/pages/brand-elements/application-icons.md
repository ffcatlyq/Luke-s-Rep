Designed to ensure uniformity and clarity, the presented guidelines serve as an extensive resource for Roche Application icons. The following page acts as a reference point for understanding the core principles underlying these icons and provides a comprehensive outline for creating new ones.

For UI icons, please refer to the [design systems](/r/48hQO3ZpCVjvFC3NyJ0ksV-CzbIkksmCSvhkeDUsbow).
Adhere to these principles when designing:

Focus on simplicity
Complex and intricate icons can be difficult to comprehend, defeating the purpose of using icons to simplify information. Simple and straightforward icons are more easily understood and emotionally impactful.

Literal symbols
The use of clear and literal symbols makes icons more relatable and emotionally engaging. Aim to use symbols that are immediately recognisable, rather than abstract metaphors.

Familiarity
In the case of an existing application, support the user by incorporating elements that feel familiar. If applicable and relevant, consider retaining the old application icon or certain elements of it during the redesign process. Nevertheless, there are scenarios where a complete redesign starting from scratch can be a more preferable approach.

Uniqueness
Besides being simple and easily understood, it is equally crucial to incorporate noticeable distinctions among the icons to ensure that users can promptly identify and distinguish between multiple applications.

---

Prior to commencing your icon design, always analyse existing icons first and periodically review the design process to avoid creating a remarkably similar icon to what already exists. Strive for simplicity and avoid unnecessary details. Ensure the main message is clear and easily recognisable.

**When dealing with complex applications, concentrate on representing a singular key function, as an icon cannot encompass the entire application’s functionality.**

The grid system provided below consists of four essential components: the pixel grid, keylines, Roche diagonals, and the safety zone. A detailed explanation of each element follows.
To ensure compliance with the guidelines, a downloadable grid template is provided that includes keylines, specifically designed for use in Adobe Illustrator and Figma.
Base grid and padding
While the overall dimensions of the asset is 512x512 pixels, the usable space is limited to 384x384 pixels, with a padding of 16 pixels. Only under exceptional circumstances, such as maintaining optical balance, can elements be placed in the padding area.

Additionally, it is essential to prioritise the easy recognition and legibility of icons at various sizes, including 192 px, 96 px, 48 px, 24 px, and 16 px (although there are more sizes available, only a few have been highlighted for reference).
Keylines
Keyline shapes in combination with grid lines provides a structured and consistent approach to icon design. The four main shapes - circle, square, portrait rectangle, and landscape rectangle - serve as the foundation for maintaining visual proportion and consistency across the entire icon set.
In cases where the new icon or core concept cannot be accurately represented through a single form, the circle shape should serve as the default keyline shape.
Anatomy
Icons are primarily constructed using solid shapes that can be enhanced with lines. These lines consistently have a thickness of 16 pixels and employ a “Round Cap” style with “Round Joins”. To ensure visual clarity and prevent clutter in areas where multiple lines intersect, it is necessary to maintain a minimum clear space of 16 pixels between lines and standalone shapes.

Special attention should be paid to lines, as they tend to be more difficult to comprehend at smaller sizes and can introduce complexity to the appearance of the icons.
Radii & the Roche angle
The design should feature rounded corners on all sides of the icon. The default rounding is 32 px, but additional options such as 24 px, 16 px, 12 px, and 8 px may be applied based on the icon and its details. The use of sharp 90-degree corners is not allowed, and make sure that the smallest rounding used is at least 8 px.

Use the 49° angle inspired by the Roche logo wherever feasible, but do not feel limited to it. Ideally, the horizontal axis should align with the 49° angle as represented in the Roche logo, but applying the same to the vertical axis is also allowed.
Stay pixel-perfect
To maintain a sharp appearance, ensure that shapes and points are aligned to the grid. To simplify the process of achieving pixel-perfect touchpoints in later stages, it is recommended to have shapes and lines with dimensions divisible by 8.
Modifiers
To avoid cluttering icons with too much detail, a “modifier” system has been created. This way, a complex idea can be visualised with a basic icon and a modifier element. The dimensions of the modifier is 144x144 pixels.

Two types of modifiers exist: one typically located in the bottom right corner (although it can be positioned elsewhere if it suits the design better), while the other situated inside the icon. The former always adopts the darkest colour of the brand and features a white symbol within it, while in the latter case, the circle is white and the additional symbol inside it is coloured (usually mid-tone).
Safe area
An integral component of the icon design, the safe zone is responsible for keeping the optimal distance between the application icon and the enclosing shape. Its purpose is to prevent any distortion or cropping of the icon’s visual elements.

A gradient background is also applied, filling the entire 512x512 pixel area. Each brand incorporates its own background, which will be further explained in the upcoming section.
Colours and background gradients
Each brand has its distinct set of colours that differentiate them from one another, and it is important to maintain the integrity of these colours by refraining from mixing them. The background gradient is also unique for each brand and runs diagonally from the top left to the bottom right at a 49° angle.

The colours on the ratio wheels should always be presented in a similar fashion, with the dominant colour on the left and the other colours serving as complementary shades. However, the most critical factor to consider is maintaining a strong contrast. Typically, it is recommended to select the most dominant colour and the darkest brand colour as the primary choice, while also considering the use of white and the lightest brand colour in certain scenarios.

Including all three colours from the wheel (excluding white) is not mandatory and should not be forced. It is only necessary to showcase the three brand colours when there are three distinct shapes or icon details that specifically call for it.

Export & touchpoints
Icons must be exported in various formats to suit different platforms. To ensure optimal image quality, all icons must be adjusted and made pixel-perfect. Icons smaller than 40 px, 32 px, or 24 px should be visually simplified, taking into account its level of detail.
A [PDF](https://branding.roche.com/share/ti8BDe6qDL7wYYLvEnU3) has been created to outline the size, format, and background style (square or rounded) for each platform. Whenever in doubt, seek guidance from the Product Owner of the application as they can provide clarity on the platforms where the icon needs to be created.

The requested naming protocol for these files is:** brand-name_application-name_platform_default/rounded.png/.svg**
Usage
There are two different versions of the icons based on their backgrounds. One version includes a background, while the other version offers icons without any background.

**It is recommended to primarily display the application icons with rounded corners and a gradient background that matches the brand’s visual identity.** However, it’s worth noting that platforms like Google Play, App Store, navify Portal, and navify Marketplace require uploading the versions without rounded corners and with gradient backgrounds. These platforms have their own mechanisms to automatically apply the desired rounding.
Usage of icons with background
When working with gradient background icons, the available colour choices are limited to three: White, Neutral 4, and Grey 5. To ensure optimal contrast between the application icon and its background, it is required to use a drop shadow with the Neutral 4 and Grey 5 colours. Adding a drop shadow on a white background is optional.
The drop shadow properties were initially set for the default size of 512x512 pixels. As the icon size varies, the shadow should be adjusted proportionally to decrease or increase accordingly.

**When dealing with icons smaller than 64x64 pixels**, maintain the ‘Y Offset’ and ‘Blur’ values of the drop shadow at a minimum of 1 pixel, without any further decrease.
Usage of icons without background
The version without a background is intended for exclusive use within the applications.

Following the same principles as the gradient background icons, the ones without a background are limited to the specified 3 colours (White, Neutral 4, Grey 5) to ensure visibility. Nevertheless, it is crucial to avoid applying any drop shadow in this particular scenario.
**Make sure there's always a safe area around the icons** to prevent anything from getting too close or squeezed.
