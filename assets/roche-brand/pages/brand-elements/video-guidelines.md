With moving imagery, we capture the same attributes as with our still picture language. Our content should connect in an authentic, emotional and relatable way with our audiences. Our moving imagery should always be elegant, uncluttered, warm, human-centred, simple and engaging.

Topic-related content can be difficult to visualise, here we can be more metaphorical with our choice of visual content. As we have limited time to reach our audiences, keep in mind that being succinct is key.

For the best accessibility, please use closed captions
‘Closed captions’ can be turned on or off by viewers and can even be shown in different languages if multiple .SRT (Subrip subtitle) files are uploaded with your video. Because of ongoing individual platform updates, we cannot list which platforms support this, so we recommend checking online for the most up-to-date instructions on the relevant sites.

‘Open captions’, on the other hand, are always visible. They are ‘burned' into the video and cannot be turned off. There can be instances where duplication of automated captions and burned-in captions clash on top of each other, making for a poor accessibility experience. We recommend that burned-in captions only be used on platforms that do not support closed captions. In some cases where the same video is uploaded to multiple platforms, you may need to create two video versions of your video – one version with closed captions and another version with open captions – and uploading the appropriate version to each platform.

Accurate captions are essential for making video content accessible. Automated captions often contain errors that can mislead or confuse users, and should not be considered a viable option. Captions should either be created manually or manually reviewed and edited for accuracy, targeting as high accuracy as possible.

Why the purpose statement appears in video content
The inclusion of the Roche signature outro [doing now what patients need next](https://branding.roche.com/document/27#/about-the-brand/purpose-statement) to our video content brings the audience’s attention to our central focus. This is the only time the external audience will see our purpose statement, forgetting to include it is not an option.

 

While storytelling is ever important to our channels, with the adapted guidelines we help you make more impact, especially when dealing with shorter duration content.

Please follow these rules when creating video content:

Longer video content over 90-seconds
will always require the outro to include the Roche purpose statement. New and updated templates with added flexibility for your needs will be launched in due course.

Shorter video content under 90-seconds
This applies also to cutdowns as well as paid social media assets – requires the Roche logo to appear for a minimum of 1-second, within the first 3 seconds of the video starting. This ensures that our audiences recognise who the content is from, building our brand strength and reputation without losing impact.

YouTube, Roche channel-specific video content
No matter the duration of the video asset published on the corporate Roche YouTube channel, the Roche purpose statement signature outro needs to be included.
