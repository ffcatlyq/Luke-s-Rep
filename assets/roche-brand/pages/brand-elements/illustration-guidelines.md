These guidelines were created to provide a standardised and well-articulated approach for the usage of illustrations in Roche communications. The following page provides comprehensive guidance on the Roche-compliant illustration style, including its key features, types, and usage, as well as a template to brief external partners on creating bespoke illustrations.

Illustrations are like little windows into ideas, stories, and concepts. They speak a language that’s simple, engaging, and easy to understand. While they don’t take the place of photographs or videos in our communications, they bring a special and thoughtful touch to topics that are difficult to capture with a camera or video. Illustrations help us tackle complex subjects, stir up emotions, and make special connections through clever metaphors. They also give our brand a distinct personality, helping us stand out in a crowded world. When we create illustrations, we keep these important principles in mind.

Consistent

Achieving consistency in illustrations is critical to maintaining the quality of the artwork and the image of the brand it represents. When consistent illustrations are utilised across all mediums, it creates a coherent and memorable image, leaving a lasting impression on the viewers.

Focused

When illustrations are created with a specific topic focus, they become more powerful and effective in communicating their intended message. This targeted approach enables the visuals to convey the message in a clear and simple manner, guaranteeing that the visuals resonate with the audience.

Considerate

It is essential to consider the intended audience, context, and the numerous cultural, geographic, gender, age, and other specificities that can affect the interpretation of illustrations. By accounting for these factors, we can ensure that the illustrations are used in a manner that aligns with their intended purpose.

Developed for Roche, the ‘clear and graphic’ style of illustration is marked by its minimalist design, bold shapes, and use of Roche brand colours. The illustrations feature simple yet smart compositions, with a focus on geometric shapes and patterns.

By employing a limited colour palette, typically focusing on two or three primary colours, the ‘clear and graphic’ style of illustration simplifies complex concepts and translates them into visually compelling compositions. Its boldness, clarity, and playful use of colour and shape make it highly engaging for audiences across the board.

Key attributes of the style include:
The ‘clear and graphic’ style of illustration incorporates the use of brand colours, including warmer tones and a reduced use of blue. The illustrations are built from simple and uneven shapes, without complex shapes or objects. Gradients and textures should not be used, and the visuals are simplified to the basics. The style avoids weird camera angles or distortion, and there are no detailed facial features.

Hero and spot illustrations are the two primary types of illustrations used. Hero illustrations work for complex topics needing more details, while spot illustrations are for simpler subjects without a background scene. Spot illustrations are standalone visuals not bounded by frame, while hero illustrations usually have a bleed to the edge.
Metaphorical
Metaphors play a central role in the ‘clear and graphic’ illustration style, acting as a powerful means to foster a deeper connection with the audience. By transforming complex concepts into relatable visual narratives, metaphors enable viewers to see themselves reflected within the story. They simplify intricate ideas into a single, emotionally resonant visual, making scientific content feel personal, accessible, and meaningful. This technique is more than a stylistic preference - it’s a fundamental element of the approach, ensuring clarity and emotional impact in every illustration.

Clean with reduced details
Clean illustrations feature simple shapes and lines with reduced details, making the message of the illustration clear and easy to comprehend while avoiding unnecessary distractions. Avoid cluttering the illustrations with too many elements or details.

Authentic and engaging
Engaging and authentic illustrations often tell a story. By incorporating characters, settings, and other narrative elements, the illustrations can draw the audience in and create a memorable visual experience.

Emotive and empathetic
Illustrations can be highly emotive and empathetic through the use of colour, composition, style, expression, and context. The combination of these elements can create powerful images that evoke different emotions and feelings.
