---

By blending static drawings with dynamic animation, animated illustrations offer an enhanced approach to storytelling, ideal for communicating complex ideas, evoke emotions, and leave a lasting impression.

To learn more about creating animated illustrations, refer to the resource provided on this page.
Adhere to these principles when designing:

Roche’s animated illustrations employ a ‘clear and graphic’ style that is brought to life through minimalist and bold shapes, as well as expressive characters. As a result, a cohesive visual style is achieved, seamlessly integrating visual elements and motion.

The animations are designed to present or expand upon the story without unnecessary decorative movements, focusing on enhancing the illustrations and highlighting a single action. While incorporating familiar animation principles like squash and stretch, anticipation, and overlapping action, it's important to avoid excessive cartoon exaggeration.

Consistent
Consistency plays a vital role in conveying professionalism and attention to detail. Plus, it ensures a seamless viewing experience, enabling the audience to effortlessly follow the narrative without any distractions or confusion.

Simple & Effortless
In order to align with the illustrations’ simple style, the animations should adopt a similar approach. The emphasis should be on keeping the movements uncomplicated and delicate, ensuring that the message remains easily understandable.

Dynamic
A well-executed animation involves the seamless movement of multiple elements, collaborating harmoniously to shape the narrative. The dynamics of these movements hold considerable influence over the mood and tone of the story.

Down to earth
In animation, incorporating elements of reality is key to achieving convincing movements. Drawing inspiration from real footage or even physically acting out the scene can provide valuable insights and enhance the authenticity of the animation.
> **[warning]** Please keep in mind that these guidelines describe animating single illustrations into a 8-10s loop. If you are looking to animate a longer video, please refrain from using this 'clear and graphic' illustration style - instead, create the animated video in a personal, human and warm style that is appropriate for the topic and target audience.

Always make sure that all versions have been correctly exported.
Modifying illustrations
To maintain a smooth and engaging narrative flow within the animation, it is possible to make adjustments to the opening and closing phases (static phases) in comparison to the original illustration.

As demonstrated in the example below, the animation showcases the character’s hand starting from a different position, employs a dotted line to smoothly guide the viewer’s attention along the path, and introduces the final destination flag during the animation. This approach ensures that the story begins by presenting the problem rather than immediately revealing the solution.

Nonetheless, it is important to approach the addition of new elements with care, using them only when necessary to drive the story forward while maintaining overall coherence.
Duration and looping
he full-length animation is **10 seconds** long and operates at **24 fps**, consisting of **three stages**:

**﻿Phase 1: Static I.** From 0s to 1s. The animation begins with a display of the illustration’s original appearance.*

**Phase 2: Animation** From 1s to 9s. The animation transpires over a duration of 8 seconds and consistently returns to its starting position.

**Phase 3: Static II.** From 9s to 10s. To create seamless loops, the ending state of the animation must match the initial static phase exactly.

Attention must be paid to ensuring that the starting and ending points of the illustration animation match precisely, as any failure to do so can result in a noticeable glitch during playback.
Easings
Easings are an integral aspect of animation as they introduce a natural and flowing quality to transitions. Applying Ease In/Ease Out or Easy Ease on keyframes guarantees a smooth and desirable overall impression.

Although Keyframe Incoming and Outgoing Velocity do not have exact values, it is recommended to strive for gentle and positive movements.

Emphasize the story
Maintain a strong focus on the narrative, ensuring that the primary movements always drive the story forward.
Echo life
When the animation is firmly rooted in real events and portrayed through realistic movements and behaviours, it establishes a strong sense of relatability and authenticity.
Offset
To achieve a fluid appearance in the animation, use offsets between the various elements or movements in relation to one another.
Depth
Add depth to the elements of the illustration, thereby creating more dynamic settings and movements that add an exciting dimension to the visuals.
Mixed mediums
Similar to how spot illustrations cannot be incorporated into photographs, real video footage cannot be mixed with animated illustrations under any circumstances.
Effects
When working with the final main composition, it is essential to consistently apply the ‘Posterize Time’ effect.

It aims to give the movements a slightly hand-animated appearance rather than a computer-generated one. To achieve this desired effect, the frame rate value of the ‘Posterize Time’ effect should be adjusted to half of the animation’s frame rate, which is equivalent to 12 fps. As a result, the animation remains at 24 fps, but only every other frame is displayed, aligning with the traditional “animating on 2s” technique employed in hand-drawn animation. In addition to altering the movement aesthetics, this approach also has the advantage of reducing the file size of the exported files.

When incorporating additional effects during animation, only those that seamlessly integrate within the visual aesthetics of the illustrations should be considered. **Motion blur and other blur effects should not be employed under any circumstances.**
Export formats & settings
Export the final animations as GIFs with an infinite loop and H.264 .mp4 formats (without the audio channel).
